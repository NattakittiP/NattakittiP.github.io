About

I am a Thai undergraduate researcher studying in the International Bachelor Program in Informatics (IBPI) at Yuan Ze University, Taiwan. My research centers on trustworthy AI, machine learning reliability, and scientific reproducibility, with a fundamental interest in understanding not only how intelligent systems perform, but when their results and the evidence behind them can truly be trusted.

My journey began in Artificial Intelligence Engineering at Satit Prasarnmit Demonstration School, where I developed a Thai-language NLP chatbot and placed 5th nationally in the World Robot Olympiad (WRO). I later spent two years studying medicine at Rangsit University's College of Medicine. This experience deepened my understanding of biomedical science and ultimately inspired my transition into informatics, where I could combine scientific reasoning with data-driven and computational approaches.

My current work investigates the reliability of machine learning through protocol-level auditing, data leakage prevention, model-selection stability, calibration, explainability, and robustness under distribution shift. Much of my research applies these methods to clinical and biomedical AI, using synthetic benchmarks and real-world datasets to examine how evaluation decisions influence scientific conclusions.

Beyond biomedical AI, my research extends to agentic LLM reliability, human-centred AI, and the philosophy of scientific trust, including distributed verification and epistemic dependence. Across these areas, I pursue a common goal: making AI systems and scientific evidence more rigorous, transparent, and dependable, so they can support better decisions in healthcare, science, and society.

Protocol-Level Auditing of Machine Learning Evaluation: Disentangling Split Policy, Leakage, and Decision Stability (2026)

Accepted after double-blind peer review and selected for publication and oral presentation at
The 23rd International Joint Conference on Computer Science and Software Engineering (JCSSE 2026)
Supervised by Dr. Qazi Mazhar ul Haq (Assistant Professor, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Yuan Ze University)

This study introduces a protocol-level audit framework to examine how data splitting, leakage, and random seeds affect machine learning model selection. Across 260 configurations and 1,300 evaluations, XGBoost remained the top-ranked model, despite measurable performance shifts and degradation under missingness. The findings demonstrate that seed-based reproducibility does not guarantee methodological validity, highlighting the importance of rigorous protocol auditing in trustworthy AI.

A Multi-World Synthetic Benchmark for Evaluating RSCE in Clinical Machine Learning (2026)

The Journal manuscript has been published in IEEE Access (IEEE-INST ELECTRICAL ELECTRONICS ENGINEERS INC, Q2)
Supervised by Dr. Qazi Mazhar ul Haq (Assistant Professor, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Yuan Ze University) and Dr. Yi-Jheng Huang (Associate Professor, Director of International Bachelor Program in Informatics, Yuan Ze University)

This study introduces a seven-world synthetic benchmark for evaluating clinical machine learning through Reliability, Robustness, Calibration Stability, and Explainability Stability (RSCE). It systematically stress-tests models under distribution shift, missingness, measurement noise, surrogate corruption, nonlinear distortion, and label noise.

Across 15,000+ experimental configurations, tree-based ensembles demonstrated consistently balanced integrity, while models with similar baseline accuracy showed substantial differences under stress. External evaluations on NHANES and MIMIC-IV showed qualitatively consistent degradation patterns, supporting RSCE as a reproducible framework for pre-deployment model reliability assessment.

A Leakage-Controlled Machine Learning Framework for Postprandial Triglyceride Phenotyping Using Synthetic Clinical Data (2026)

The Journal manuscript has been published in Scientific Reports (NATURE PORTFOLIO, Q1)
Supervised by Dr. Qazi Mazhar ul Haq (Assistant Professor, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Yuan Ze University) and Dr. Yi-Jheng Huang (Associate Professor, Director of International Bachelor Program in Informatics, Yuan Ze University)

This study develops a leakage-controlled machine learning framework using 1,500 synthetic clinical records to investigate the role of whole blood viscosity (WBV) in postprandial triglyceride response. The framework integrates nested cross-validation, probability calibration, robustness testing, and SHAP explainability.

The model achieved an AUROC of 0.914, identifying fasting triglycerides as the dominant predictor, while WBV showed negligible predictive contribution under the synthetic assumptions. The findings highlight the value of reproducible evaluation and transparent null results in trustworthy biomedical machine learning.

Preprint

Can MIMIC-IV Demo Support Model Selection? An Audit Against Full Data (2026)

Preprint available on ResearchGate
Supervised by Dr. Qazi Mazhar ul Haq (Assistant Professor, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Yuan Ze University) and Dr. Yi-Jheng Huang (Associate Professor, Director of International Bachelor Program in Informatics, Yuan Ze University)

This study investigates whether MIMIC-IV and MIMIC-IV-ED Demo datasets can support reliable machine learning model selection. Using identical evaluation protocols on Demo and Full releases, it compares seven classifiers through patient-grouped cross-validation, ten-world RSCE benchmarking, and robustness analysis.

Across both clinical tasks, all seven models performed worse on Demo, with weak ranking agreement and different top-ranked models. Demo-sized samples recovered the Full-best model in only 2.8–10.7% of cases. The findings demonstrate that reproducing a benchmark workflow does not guarantee reproducible scientific conclusions, highlighting the risks of model selection using small clinical datasets.

Delay or Failure? Network Impairment in Multi-Call LLM Workflows (2026)

Preprint available on ResearchGate
Supervised by Dr. Qazi Mazhar ul Haq (Assistant Professor, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Yuan Ze University) and Dr. Yi-Jheng Huang (Associate Professor, Director of International Bachelor Program in Informatics, Yuan Ze University; Corresponding Author).
Conducted in research collaboration with the National Electronics and Computer Technology Center (NECTEC), Thailand, involving Ekkachan Rattanalerdnusorn and Dr. Chalee Vorakulpipat.

This study investigates how network impairment affects the reliability of multi-call LLM workflows using controlled packet loss, delay, and deadline experiments. A telemetry-based evaluation framework separates model-serving time, experimental waiting, and residual overhead to distinguish network-induced delays from workflow failures.

All 240 workflows completed under 0–10% packet loss, despite increasing tail latency. Under severe loss, completion declined from 34/40 for short responses to 9/40 for long responses. The findings demonstrate that response length, network conditions, and deadline constraints jointly determine workflow reliability, informing more rigorous evaluation of networked agentic AI systems.

Trust Without Knowing: Knowbind and Distributed Verification in Scientific Publishing (2026)

Single-author preprint available on PhilPapers
Sole Author & Corresponding Author: Nattakitti Piyavechvirat (International Bachelor Program in Informatics, Yuan Ze University, Taiwan).

This study introduces Knowbind, a conceptual framework examining how researchers should rely on scientific claims they cannot independently verify before critical decisions. It distinguishes the necessity of proceeding from epistemic justification, arguing for calibrated, defeasible reliance grounded in available evidence rather than unquestioned trust.

The work develops a seven-field distributed verification framework tracking scientific accountability, verification evidence, and its limitations. Through structured philosophical analysis and comparative cases, it shows how Knowbind and distributed verification clarify responsible scientific decisions under uncertainty. The findings offer a foundation for more transparent, accountable, and evidence-sensitive scientific publishing.
